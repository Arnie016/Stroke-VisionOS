# Spatial case workspace

## Promise

Stroke Care gives a clinician and a patient or family one shared spatial map for
one difficult conversation. The room teaches; side surfaces only control pace,
questions, and wording boundaries.

## Interaction grammar

1. Pull one fictional case file from the shelf. Its three relevant signals
   unfold progressively: speech, arm, and reported time.
2. Choose the current headset role. Family questions park on the left;
   presenter-only controls park on the right.
3. The file remains pinned beside the anatomy. A clot reticle and short spatial
   annotation point at what changed. The user can orbit, scale, or tap the clot.
4. A family wearer can place a question marker on the anatomy. The presenter
   sees a clarification cue, not inferred anxiety or physiology.
5. With explicit permission, the model separates skull, dura, and brain using a
   calm zip-like reveal. There is no incision or blood effect.

## Depth system

The story keeps one stable hierarchy:

`case file -> brain -> artery -> clot -> pressure-purpose layers`

The pinned file is the drawer; the central anatomy is the content; the clot
reticle is the current focus; the breadcrumb only preserves nested context.
There is no permanent catalog of unrelated anatomy parts.

## Spatial intent

- The case drawer stays left of the anatomy and carries only three visual facts.
- The brain owns the centre and largest visual mass.
- The presenter rail stays outside the patient's shared focal field.
- The family surface stays compact and offers only pause, clarification, and
  point-on-brain actions.
- Paper Shader motion remains an atmospheric horizon. It never represents
  blood, perfusion, pressure, or simulation output.
- Vessel flow is qualitative teaching motion, not CFD, velocity, collateral
  flow, or patient measurement.

## Safety and proof boundaries

`CASE-078` is fictional. UI role separation is not authentication, privacy
isolation, multi-user access control, or a medical-record system. The current
copy remains clinician-review pending. A Simulator build proves compilation;
an immersive screenshot requires a user-initiated open; XCAT comfort,
legibility, gesture reliability, and clinical usefulness require separate
human tests.
