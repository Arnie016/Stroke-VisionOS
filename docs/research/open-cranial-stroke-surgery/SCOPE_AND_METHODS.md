# Scope and methods

## Review question

What evidence and communication guidance should ground a clinician-mediated visual tool for urgent conversations with families about open cranial operations after ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage, cerebral venous thrombosis, or hemorrhage from a vascular malformation?

## Search date and sources

The search was performed through **8 August 2026**. It used PubMed/MEDLINE records, PubMed Central full text, professional-society guideline sites, NICE, AHRQ, the US Department of Health and Human Services, the General Medical Council, the American College of Surgeons, and journal/publisher pages. Citation chaining from major guidelines and systematic reviews was used to locate landmark trials and important cohorts.

Representative search concepts included combinations of:

```text
(stroke OR cerebral infarction OR intracerebral hemorrhage OR subarachnoid
 hemorrhage OR cerebral venous thrombosis OR arteriovenous malformation)
AND
(decompressive craniectomy OR hemicraniectomy OR craniotomy OR clot evacuation
 OR suboccipital decompression OR aneurysm clipping OR hematoma evacuation)
AND
(randomized OR guideline OR systematic review OR prognosis OR quality of life
 OR shared decision making OR family communication OR informed consent)
```

Targeted searches were also run for DECIMAL, DESTINY, HAMLET, STICH, SWITCH, ENRICH, MISTIE, CLEAR, ISAT, BRAT, cerebellar infarction, cerebellar hemorrhage, cranioplasty, syndrome of the trephined, teach-back, decision aids, neuroprognostication, cultural and language access, and ICU family communication.

## Inclusion criteria

- Current or historically practice-shaping professional guidelines and scientific statements.
- Randomized trials of open cranial surgery and of relevant minimally invasive/endovascular comparators.
- Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, individual-participant-data analyses, and high-value prospective or multicenter observational studies.
- Cohorts addressing rare operations for which randomized evidence is absent.
- Studies of patient/surrogate experience, neurocritical-care prognostication, shared decision-making, decision aids, informed consent, risk communication, interpreter use, or teach-back.
- Sources with enough bibliographic information to provide a stable DOI, PubMed, PMC, society, or government link.

## Exclusion and boundary rules

- Trauma, tumors, infections, epilepsy surgery, and elective non-stroke craniotomy.
- Carotid endarterectomy: it is open vascular surgery in the neck, not an open cranial operation.
- Stand-alone thrombectomy, thrombolysis, aneurysm coiling, and stereotactic/endoscopic evacuation except as alternatives or comparators needed for an accurate decision explanation.
- Animal, cadaver, and technical simulation studies unless directly relevant to product visualization; none are used to support patient-outcome claims here.
- Unverifiable secondary summaries, marketing pages, and unsourced patient-education content.

## Evidence labels used in this library

| Label | Meaning | Appropriate product use |
|---|---|---|
| **Guideline** | Formal professional recommendation, with its own evidence rating | Default source for indication language; preserve recommendation strength and jurisdiction. |
| **Randomized trial** | Prospective allocation to treatment groups | Explain trial-population benefits and harms; do not individualize the effect. |
| **Systematic review / IPD meta-analysis** | Structured synthesis, sometimes using participant-level data | Summarize consistency, heterogeneity, and gaps across studies. |
| **Observational comparative** | Non-randomized comparison, susceptible to treatment-selection and survivor bias | Show as association, never causation. |
| **Case series / registry** | Descriptive experience without a reliable counterfactual | Use to show feasibility, complications, and ranges—not efficacy. |
| **Consensus / communication standard** | Expert or policy guidance, sometimes supported by implementation evidence | Use for workflow and language patterns, with local legal adaptation. |
| **Emerging / investigational** | Insufficiently validated for routine practice | Hide from the default family pathway or display with an explicit research label. |

## How each source was summarized

Every annotated record in [SOURCE_SUMMARIES.md](SOURCE_SUMMARIES.md) contains:

- the clinical or communication question;
- population and design;
- result or recommendation most relevant to the product;
- limitation that prevents overgeneralization;
- a safe family-facing translation or product implication.

Numbers are included only when they materially affect understanding. They retain the study's endpoint and time horizon. “Favorable,” “unfavorable,” “independent,” and modified Rankin Scale cutoffs are not treated as interchangeable.

## Important limitations

This is a **curated evidence map, not a registered systematic review**. “All related surgeries” is operationalized as comprehensive coverage of the major stroke-related open cranial pathways, not a claim that every published paper in every language has been captured. No duplicate screening, formal risk-of-bias instrument, GRADE reassessment, or quantitative meta-analysis was performed. Several rare and rescue operations have only retrospective evidence. Even randomized trials enrolled selected patients and may not represent very old, frail, highly comorbid, late-presenting, anticoagulated, or anatomically unusual patients.

Guidelines can differ because they ask different questions, use different evidence cutoffs, and serve different health systems. The date, society, recommendation strength, and underlying evidence must remain attached to any statement used in the product.

## Update and quality-control protocol

Before content is promoted from research to the application:

1. A clinician verifies every citation against the linked full text or official guideline.
2. A second reviewer checks population, intervention, comparator, endpoint, denominator, time horizon, and limitation.
3. The product records a content version, reviewer names/roles, review date, and next-review date.
4. A scheduled search checks for guideline updates, new randomized trials, corrections, retractions, and safety communications at least every six months.
5. A major guideline or pivotal trial triggers an out-of-cycle review.
6. Superseded content is retained in an audit log but removed from the clinical-facing default.
7. Links are checked automatically; evidence interpretation is never updated automatically without clinical review.
