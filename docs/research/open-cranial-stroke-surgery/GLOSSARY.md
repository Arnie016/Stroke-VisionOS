# Glossary and outcome measures

## Operative and anatomical terms

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|
| Aneurysm | A weakened bulge in an artery that can rupture and bleed. |
| Aphasia | Difficulty producing or understanding language; it is not the same as lack of intelligence. |
| Arteriovenous malformation (AVM) | An abnormal tangle that connects arteries directly to veins. |
| Brainstem compression | Pressure on structures controlling consciousness, breathing, movement, and other vital functions. |
| Cerebellum | Brain region in the lower back of the skull important for coordination and balance. |
| Cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT) | A clot in veins/sinuses that drain blood from the brain. |
| Craniectomy | Removal of skull bone that is intentionally not replaced during the same operation. |
| Cranioplasty | Later reconstruction of a skull defect with preserved bone or an implant. |
| Craniotomy | Temporary removal of a bone flap that is usually replaced during the same operation. |
| Decompression | Creating space or reducing pressure; it does not by itself repair injured brain. |
| Dura / duraplasty | Tough covering of the brain / opening and expanding that covering with a patch. |
| External ventricular drain (EVD) | A catheter that drains cerebrospinal fluid from a brain ventricle and can monitor pressure. |
| Hematoma | A collection of blood/clot. |
| Hemicraniectomy | A large craniectomy over one side of the cerebral hemisphere. |
| Herniation | Dangerous displacement of brain tissue caused by pressure. |
| Hydrocephalus | Abnormal build-up of cerebrospinal fluid, often from blocked flow. |
| Infarction | Tissue injury/death from inadequate blood flow. |
| Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) | Bleeding within brain tissue. |
| Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) | Blood within the brain's fluid-filled ventricles. |
| Mass effect | Compression or shift caused by swelling, clot, or another space-occupying process. |
| Midline shift | Displacement of central brain structures to one side. |
| Necrosectomy / strokectomy | Removal of infarcted tissue; a selected or investigational technique depending on context. |
| Posterior fossa | Tight skull compartment containing the cerebellum and brainstem. |
| Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) | Bleeding into the fluid-filled space around the brain, often from a ruptured aneurysm. |
| Suboccipital craniectomy | Removal of bone at the lower back of the skull to relieve posterior-fossa pressure. |
| Supratentorial | Above the tentorium, mainly the cerebral hemispheres. |

## Modified Rankin Scale (mRS)

The mRS is a global disability scale common in stroke trials. Definitions are condensed below; a trained assessment follows a validated interview and context. The scale does not fully capture aphasia, cognition, mood, fatigue, identity, participation, caregiver burden, or quality of life.

| Score | Conventional description | Family-facing caution |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | No symptoms | Does not mean the person has no subtle cognitive or emotional effects. |
| 1 | Symptoms without significant disability; usual activities possible | “Usual activities” varies by person. |
| 2 | Slight disability; independent in own affairs but unable to perform all previous activities | Often called “independent,” despite real limitations. |
| 3 | Moderate disability; needs some help but walks without another person's assistance | Can include major language/cognitive limitations not conveyed by walking. |
| 4 | Moderately severe disability; unable to walk or attend to bodily needs without help | Assistance needs and communication abilities vary widely. |
| 5 | Severe disability; bedridden, incontinent, requiring constant nursing care and attention | Quality of life cannot be assumed from the score alone. |
| 6 | Death | Must be displayed separately rather than merged with severe disability. |

Trials variously call mRS 0–2, 0–3, or 0–4 “favorable,” and mRS 4–6 or 5–6 “unfavorable.” Those labels encode different value judgments. The product must show the actual cutoff and preferably the full distribution.

## Evidence and decision terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolute risk | The number/proportion with an outcome in a defined group and time, e.g. 33 in 100 by six months. |
| Relative risk | A ratio between groups; it can look large while hiding the starting risk and must not be shown alone. |
| Confidence interval | A range reflecting sampling uncertainty around an estimate, not the full range of possible individual outcomes. |
| Confounding by indication | Sicker, anatomically different, or more salvageable patients are selected for treatment, distorting non-randomized comparisons. |
| Decision aid | A tool that supports understanding of options and values; it does not choose or provide consent by itself. |
| Evidence certainty | Confidence that a result reflects the true effect for the specified question, not a grade of how important the operation feels. |
| Material risk | A risk a reasonable person in the patient's position would consider significant, plus risks this patient would consider significant; legal wording varies. |
| Natural frequency | A count such as “20 out of 100,” generally easier to compare than percentages with different denominators. |
| Population estimate | A study-group result; not an individualized forecast. |
| Shared decision-making | Clinician expertise and the patient's informed values jointly shape a decision; it does not mean the clinician withholds a recommendation. |
| Substituted judgment | A surrogate represents what the patient would choose, using known wishes and values; legal standards vary. |
| Teach-back | Asking someone to explain information in their own words to test and improve the clinician's explanation. |
| Time-limited trial | A defined treatment period with explicit goals, reassessment time, and agreed next actions; not suitable for postponing every time-critical decision. |
| Utility-weighted mRS | A trial endpoint assigning preference weights to mRS categories; weights are population constructs, not this patient's values. |

## “Good outcome” is not a safe standalone phrase

Every outcome statement should specify:

1. the exact outcome or mRS range;
2. the time point;
3. the study population;
4. the treatment and comparator;
5. mortality separately;
6. uncertainty and limitations; and
7. that individual recovery and acceptable quality of life cannot be read directly from the group statistic.
