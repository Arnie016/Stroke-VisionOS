# Annotated source summaries

Each record is a compact evidence card, not a substitute for reading the linked source. **RCT** means randomized controlled trial; **IPD** means individual participant data; **mRS** means modified Rankin Scale. Product language must preserve the source's population, endpoint, time horizon, evidence certainty, and limitations.

## A. Cross-cutting clinical guidelines and prognostication

### A1. Greenberg et al. — 2022 AHA/ASA spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage guideline

**Source:** *Guideline for the Management of Patients With Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage* (Stroke, 2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35579034/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000407)
**Summary:** Routine craniotomy for most supratentorial ICH has uncertain usefulness, but evacuation may be considered lifesaving in a deteriorating patient. Immediate surgery, with or without EVD, is recommended for cerebellar ICH with deterioration, brainstem compression, obstructive hydrocephalus, or volume at least 15 mL. The guideline also warns against using a severity score as the sole basis for limiting treatment and distinguishes do-not-resuscitate status from other treatment limits.
**Product use:** Default source for US indication language, with class/level retained. Show cerebellar hemorrhage separately from supratentorial hemorrhage.

### A2. Steiner et al. — 2025 ESO/EANS spontaneous ICH guideline

**Source:** *European Stroke Organisation and European Association of Neurosurgical Societies guideline on spontaneous intracerebral haemorrhage* (European Stroke Journal, 2025). [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12098356/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/23969873251340815)
**Summary:** Across 37 PICO questions and 208 studies, many surgical recommendations remain weak because certainty is very low. The panel suggests open craniotomy for selected non-comatose lobar ICH when minimally invasive surgery is unavailable, and gives a weak recommendation for decompressive craniectomy in a tightly defined severe deep-ICH population; it also addresses cerebellar hematomas over 15 mL.
**Product use:** Show recommendation strength beside every operation. Do not turn a weak suggestion into a standard-of-care banner.

### A3. van der Worp et al. — 2021 ESO guideline on space-occupying brain infarction

**Source:** *European Stroke Organisation guidelines on the management of space-occupying brain infarction* (European Stroke Journal, 2021). [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8370077/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/23969873211027001)
**Summary:** High-quality evidence supports decompressive surgery within 48 hours in selected adults aged 60 or younger with space-occupying hemispheric infarction; evidence is lower in older adults and uncertain after 48 hours. Cerebellar selection lacks randomized evidence. Expert consensus recommends a large cranial opening, at least 12 cm in diameter, to reduce complications from inadequate decompression.
**Product use:** Ground the ischemic decompression pathway and clearly label timing and age evidence gaps.

### A4. Wijdicks et al. — 2014 AHA/ASA statement on cerebral and cerebellar infarction with swelling

**Source:** *Recommendations for the management of cerebral and cerebellar infarction with swelling* (Stroke, 2014). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24481970/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.str.0000441965.15164.d6)
**Summary:** The statement covers early recognition, transfer to monitored neurocritical care, medical management, hemicraniectomy for malignant hemispheric infarction, suboccipital decompression for deteriorating cerebellar infarction, and EVD for obstructive hydrocephalus. It emphasizes discussing the possibility of survival with severe disability.
**Product use:** Use its integrated pathway to show that surgery sits inside ongoing stroke and critical care, not as a stand-alone event.

### A5. Hoh et al. — 2023 AHA/ASA aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage guideline

**Source:** *Guideline for the Management of Patients With Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage* (Stroke, 2023). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37212182/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000436)
**Summary:** Ruptured aneurysms should be evaluated by specialists with both surgical and endovascular expertise and treated promptly to achieve complete obliteration where feasible. The choice of clipping or endovascular treatment depends on clinical grade, aneurysm features, age, hemorrhage pattern, durability, and center expertise.
**Product use:** Present clipping and coiling as anatomy- and context-dependent routes, not a generic Plan A/Plan B ranking.

### A6. Treggiari et al. — 2023 Neurocritical Care Society aSAH guideline

**Source:** *Guidelines for the Neurocritical Care Management of Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage* (Neurocritical Care, 2023). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37202712/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12028-023-01713-5)
**Summary:** This multidisciplinary guideline addresses critical-care management before and after aneurysm securing, including rebleeding, hydrocephalus, seizures, delayed cerebral ischemia, and systemic complications. Evidence is limited for many ICU practices, making explicit evidence ratings important.
**Product use:** Extend the clip animation into a realistic postoperative pathway rather than ending at aneurysm closure.

### A7. Vergouwen et al. — 2026 ESO/EANS/ESMINT aSAH guideline

**Source:** *Guideline on the management of aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage* (European Stroke Journal, 2026). [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13151661/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/esj/aakag043)
**Summary:** The guideline recommends multidisciplinary selection and favors coiling over clipping for many good-condition patients when both are equally suitable. It supports immediate surgery combining hematoma removal and clipping when a patient is deteriorating from a space-occupying intracerebral or subdural hematoma. Some recommendations remain consensus-based because trials do not represent complex cases.
**Product use:** This is the most current European aSAH anchor as of the search date; attach publication date and never apply its coiling preference to every aneurysm.

### A8. Ferro et al. — 2017 ESO cerebral venous thrombosis guideline

**Source:** *European Stroke Organization guideline for the diagnosis and treatment of cerebral venous thrombosis* (European Journal of Neurology, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28833980/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.13381)
**Summary:** The guideline recommends parenteral anticoagulation in acute CVT, including cases with intracerebral hemorrhage, and decompressive surgery to prevent death from herniation. Surgical evidence is non-randomized and very low certainty.
**Product use:** Explain that anticoagulation treats thrombosis while decompression treats pressure; hemorrhage does not automatically make the two goals contradictory.

### A9. Canadian Stroke Best Practices — 2024 CVT recommendations

**Source:** *Canadian Stroke Best Practice Recommendations, 7th edition: Cerebral Venous Thrombosis, 2024 update*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38826076/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/cjn.2024.269)
**Summary:** The update covers diagnosis, acute anticoagulation, deterioration, endovascular options, decompressive surgery, long-term treatment, pregnancy, and follow-up. Decompression is a rescue option for malignant mass effect, with decisions made urgently by stroke/neurosurgical teams.
**Product use:** Use as a current North American pathway and localize drug, referral, and legal details before deployment.

### A10. Derdeyn et al. — 2017 AHA/ASA brain AVM scientific statement

**Source:** *Management of Brain Arteriovenous Malformations* (Stroke, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642352/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000134)
**Summary:** The statement reviews natural history, imaging, microsurgery, embolization, and radiosurgery for ruptured and unruptured AVMs. Ruptured-AVM treatment must integrate hematoma urgency, AVM grade/anatomy, rebleeding risk, and multimodal expertise; evidence is largely observational.
**Product use:** Keep emergency hematoma/decompression and definitive AVM obliteration as separate decisions.

### A11. Hwang et al. — 2024 NCS guideline on neuroprognostication in ICH

**Source:** *Guidelines for Neuroprognostication in Adults with Intracerebral Hemorrhage* (Neurocritical Care, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37923968/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959839/)
**Summary:** No single admission variable or score can reliably predict an individual's poor outcome with sufficient certainty. Except in the most neurologically devastated situations, the guideline supports delaying definitive prognostication, using multiple data sources, acknowledging uncertainty, and avoiding self-fulfilling early treatment limitation.
**Product use:** Block automatic “predicted outcome” displays and prompt scheduled reassessment.

### A12. Mainali et al. — 2026 NCS/DGNI guideline on neuroprognostication after acute ischemic stroke

**Source:** *Guidelines for Neuroprognostication in Adults with Acute Ischemic Stroke* (Neurocritical Care, 2026). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41942818/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13249629/)
**Summary:** Prognosis should be multimodal, conditional, and repeatedly updated; available models do not justify deterministic individual predictions. Timing, treatment effects, sedation, and limitations of outcome scales matter.
**Product use:** Require uncertainty language and disable extrapolation from a trial average to a named patient.

### A13. Creutzfeldt et al. — 2025 AHA palliative and end-of-life care after stroke statement

**Source:** *Palliative and End-of-Life Care in Stroke* (Stroke, 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39676661/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000479)
**Summary:** Palliative care is appropriate alongside life-prolonging treatment from the time of serious stroke. It includes symptom control, values-based communication, support for surrogates, and transitions when goals change; it is not synonymous with abandoning care.
**Product use:** Include comfort and family support in every branch, including surgery-directed care.

### A14. Society of Critical Care Medicine — 2024 family-centered adult ICU guideline

**Source:** *Guidelines on Family-Centered Care for Adult ICUs: 2024* (Critical Care Medicine, published 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39982184/)
**Summary:** Structured family communication, staff training, family presence/support, and attention to mental health are encouraged, although evidence for any one communication tool is often low certainty. Family-centered care requires systems and staffing, not only a visual aid.
**Product use:** Design the tool as one component of a team workflow with a named clinician, nurse, interpreter, and follow-up plan.

### A15. NICE NG197 — shared decision making

**Source:** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, *Shared decision making* (2021). [Guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197) · [Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197/chapter/Recommendations)
**Summary:** NICE recommends plain language, explicit option comparison, absolute risks, natural frequencies with the same denominator and time period, positive and negative framing, visual aids, uncertainty, and teach-back. NG197 explicitly does not govern unexpected emergencies or decisions for people lacking capacity, so local emergency/capacity law remains essential.
**Product use:** Adopt its risk-display principles, not its legal scope.

### A16. NICE NG128 evidence review — decompressive hemicraniectomy

**Source:** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, *Evidence review for decompressive hemicraniectomy* (Stroke and transient ischaemic attack in over 16s, 2019). [Evidence review PDF](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng128/evidence/h-surgery-decompressive-hemicraniectomy-pdf-6777399573)
**Summary:** This evidence review synthesizes randomized decompression trials, outcomes by age and timing, and committee interpretation for UK recommendations. It makes transparent why mortality, severe disability, and patient preferences must all inform selection.
**Product use:** Useful traceability source when translating guideline wording into a UK-facing pathway.

## B. Malignant hemispheric ischemic infarction

### B1. Vahedi et al. — DECIMAL trial

**Source:** *Sequential-design, multicenter, randomized, controlled trial of early decompressive craniectomy in malignant middle cerebral artery infarction (DECIMAL)* (Stroke, 2007). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.107.485235)
**Summary:** This small French RCT compared early hemicraniectomy with medical treatment in selected younger adults and demonstrated a large mortality reduction, although enrollment stopped before the planned sample. Functional endpoints were imprecise because of the small group.
**Product use:** Combine with the pooled trials; do not show the small-study point estimate as a personalized probability.

### B2. Jüttler et al. — DESTINY trial

**Source:** *Decompressive Surgery for the Treatment of Malignant Infarction of the Middle Cerebral Artery (DESTINY)* (Stroke, 2007). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17690310/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.107.485649)
**Summary:** In 32 selected patients aged 18–60, 30-day survival was 88% after early surgery versus 47% with conservative care. The small trial was designed around survival and cannot precisely resolve each disability category.
**Product use:** Use to explain the survival effect, paired with disability distributions from pooled evidence.

### B3. Hofmeijer et al. — HAMLET trial

**Source:** *Surgical decompression for space-occupying cerebral infarction (HAMLET)* (Lancet Neurology, 2009). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19269254/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2809%2970047-X)
**Summary:** Among 64 patients randomized up to 96 hours, surgery reduced case fatality but did not significantly improve the trial's primary functional outcome overall. Combined trial evidence favored operations within 48 hours; HAMLET did not establish benefit from waiting as long as 96 hours.
**Product use:** The decision timer must say “evidence is strongest within 48 hours,” not promise that every patient has a fixed deadline.

### B4. Vahedi et al. — pooled DECIMAL/DESTINY/HAMLET analysis

**Source:** *Early decompressive surgery in malignant infarction of the middle cerebral artery: a pooled analysis of three randomised controlled trials* (Lancet Neurology, 2007). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17303527/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422%2807%2970036-4)
**Summary:** IPD from 93 patients aged 18–60 treated within 48 hours showed surgery reduced mortality and increased survival across mRS thresholds. The analysis established practice but remains a small, highly selected dataset with outcomes dichotomized at several mRS cutoffs.
**Product use:** Show the complete ordinal outcome distribution where possible; never call all survivors “good outcomes.”

### B5. Jüttler et al. — DESTINY II

**Source:** *Hemicraniectomy in Older Patients with Extensive Middle-Cerebral-Artery Stroke* (New England Journal of Medicine, 2014). [Full text](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1311367) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311367)
**Summary:** In 112 patients aged 61 or older (median 70), six-month survival without mRS 5–6 was 38% after surgery versus 18% with medical care, and mortality was 33% versus 70%. No participant achieved mRS 0–2; most survivors needed help with most bodily needs or had moderately severe disability.
**Product use:** Always pair the survival gain with the disability distribution and ask about the patient's values concerning dependence.

### B6. Zhao et al. — decompression in patients up to age 80

**Source:** *Decompressive hemicraniectomy in malignant middle cerebral artery infarct: a randomized controlled trial enrolling patients up to 80 years old* (Neurocritical Care, 2012). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22528280/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12028-012-9703-3)
**Summary:** This small RCT broadened the age range and supported a mortality benefit, with age strongly associated with functional outcome. Sample size and single-country practice limit precise subgroup estimates.
**Product use:** It supports discussing—but not quantifying—age as one of several modifiers.

### B7. Reinink et al. — IPD meta-analysis of randomized trials

**Source:** *Surgical Decompression for Space-Occupying Hemispheric Infarction: A Systematic Review and Individual Patient Meta-analysis of Randomized Clinical Trials* (JAMA Neurology, 2021). [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7551237/)
**Summary:** IPD from 488 patients in seven RCTs confirmed that decompression reduces poor outcomes in the populations studied. Evidence was sparse for treatment started after day two and for older or otherwise underrepresented subgroups, limiting reliable interaction estimates.
**Product use:** Use as the broad trial synthesis and expose the “evidence missing” fields when a case falls outside trial criteria.

### B8. Wei et al. — randomized-trial meta-analysis

**Source:** *Decompressive hemicraniectomy versus medical treatment of malignant middle cerebral artery infarction: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (Bioscience Reports, 2020). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31854446/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6944664/)
**Summary:** Nine RCTs involving 425 patients showed a consistent mortality reduction, while functional conclusions varied by mRS definition, age, and follow-up. Meta-analysis cannot fix small trials, heterogeneity, or preference-sensitive definitions of acceptable outcome.
**Product use:** Avoid a single green “benefit” badge; label the endpoint and cutoff.

### B9. Räty et al. — dominant versus non-dominant hemisphere meta-analysis

**Source:** *Functional Outcome After Decompressive Hemicraniectomy in Patients With Dominant or Non-Dominant Malignant Middle Cerebral Infarcts* (Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases, 2021). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jstrokecerebrovasdis.2021.106102)
**Summary:** Across 51 mostly observational studies and 2,361 patients, hemisphere dominance was not consistently associated with global functional outcome. Certainty was low/very low and mRS incompletely captures language, cognition, participation, and caregiver burden.
**Product use:** Do not automatically exclude dominant-hemisphere stroke; add a language-specific values and disability discussion.

### B10. Kastrau et al. — language after dominant-hemisphere decompression

**Source:** *Recovery from aphasia after hemicraniectomy for infarction of the speech-dominant hemisphere* (Stroke, 2005). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.STR.0000157595.93115.70)
**Summary:** This small observational study documented varying language recovery after dominant-hemisphere hemicraniectomy. It counters deterministic claims that meaningful communication is impossible, but cannot predict an individual outcome.
**Product use:** Use only to demonstrate uncertainty and domains missed by mRS, not to promise speech recovery.

### B11. Neugebauer et al. — retrospective consent and attitudes

**Source:** *DESTINY-S: attitudes of physicians, patients and relatives toward disability and treatment after malignant MCA infarction* (Neurocritical Care, 2017). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12028-016-0362-7)
**Summary:** Retrospective assessments suggest survivors and families may rate outcomes differently from clinicians and from what they expected during the emergency. Survivorship, recall, adaptation, and nonresponse biases are substantial.
**Product use:** Elicit the patient's values rather than allowing the tool or clinician to assign a universal value to mRS states.

### B12. Moughal et al. — strokectomy meta-analysis

**Source:** *Strokectomy for malignant middle cerebral artery infarction: experience and meta-analysis of current evidence* (Journal of Neurology, 2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33340332/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8739160/)
**Summary:** Small non-randomized series explored resection of infarcted tissue, with or without decompression, as an alternative strategy. Pooled results were hypothesis-generating, vulnerable to selection bias, and did not establish equivalence or superiority to standard hemicraniectomy.
**Product use:** Label strokectomy “investigational/selected-center practice” and keep it out of the default recommendation path.

## C. Space-occupying cerebellar infarction

### C1. Chen et al. — early surgical case series

**Source:** *Outcome of severe cerebellar infarction after surgical decompression* (Stroke, 1992). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1615544/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.STR.23.7.957)
**Summary:** Eleven deteriorating patients underwent posterior-fossa decompression; the series demonstrated the possibility of survival and functional recovery after a condition often fatal without pressure relief. With no control group and very small numbers, it cannot quantify treatment effect or define a surgical threshold.
**Product use:** Historical feasibility evidence only.

### C2. Kim et al. — preventive suboccipital decompression

**Source:** *Preventive Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy for Cerebellar Infarction* (Stroke, 2016). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27608818/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.014078)
**Summary:** A matched retrospective comparison (28 surgical patients and 56 controls) suggested earlier “preventive” decompression may improve outcome in selected patients with large infarcts before profound deterioration. Treatment selection and residual confounding prevent causal conclusions.
**Product use:** Do not implement an imaging-volume trigger from this study; show it as an unresolved timing question.

### C3. Ayling et al. — systematic review/meta-analysis

**Source:** *Suboccipital Decompressive Craniectomy for Cerebellar Infarction: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis* (World Neurosurgery, 2018). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29104155/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2017.10.144)
**Summary:** Eleven retrospective studies (283 patients) produced pooled estimates of about 20% mortality, 28% moderate/severe disability, and 23% adverse events. Heterogeneity, publication bias, different surgical techniques, and absence of non-surgical comparators make these descriptive—not patient-predictive—rates.
**Product use:** Show outcome ranges only in an expert evidence panel with an observational-evidence warning.

### C4. Hernández-Durán et al. — necrosectomy versus decompression

**Source:** *Clinical and Radiological Outcomes of Space-Occupying Cerebellar Infarction Managed by Suboccipital Decompression With or Without Necrosectomy* (Neurosurgery, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37800900/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1227/neu.0000000000002707)
**Summary:** In a retrospective multicenter cohort of 92 patients, necrosectomy-based approaches were associated with better functional outcomes than decompression alone. Procedure choice was not randomized and likely reflected anatomy, timing, surgeon preference, and severity.
**Product use:** Present technique variation without declaring a superior method; identify the need for a randomized trial.

### C5. Suyama et al. — decompression case series

**Source:** *Evaluation of clinical significance of decompressive suboccipital craniectomy on the prognosis of cerebellar infarction* (Fujita Medical Journal, 2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35111496/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8766232/)
**Summary:** Fourteen surgically treated patients were analyzed for factors associated with outcome. Small, single-center retrospective data can illustrate presentation and course, but cannot establish a universal Glasgow Coma Scale, volume, or timing threshold.
**Product use:** Use only for expert background, not family-facing numeric claims.

### C6. 2025 conservative-versus-surgical systematic review

**Source:** *Conservative versus surgical treatment in the management of cerebellar infarction: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41204465/)
**Summary:** Across 12 heterogeneous observational studies (1,108 patients), pooled comparisons did not show a clear mortality or mRS advantage for surgery. Severe confounding by indication is likely because deteriorating and anatomically different patients are preferentially operated on.
**Product use:** Explain honestly that rescue recommendations rely on pathophysiology, natural history, and non-randomized evidence—not a definitive comparative trial.

## D. Spontaneous intracerebral and intraventricular hemorrhage

### D1. Mendelow et al. — STICH

**Source:** *Early surgery versus initial conservative treatment in patients with spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral haematomas* (Lancet, 2005). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15680453/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2805%2917826-X)
**Summary:** In 1,033 patients, routine early open surgery did not produce an overall functional benefit compared with initial conservative care. Crossover and heterogeneity leave room for selected rescue cases and superficial hematomas, but the trial rejects a simple “remove every clot” rule.
**Product use:** The default supratentorial pathway must say “routine open removal has uncertain benefit.”

### D2. Mendelow et al. — STICH II

**Source:** *Early surgery versus initial conservative treatment in patients with spontaneous supratentorial lobar intracerebral haematomas (STICH II)* (Lancet, 2013). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23726393/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906609/)
**Summary:** Among 601 conscious patients with superficial lobar ICH and no intraventricular hemorrhage, unfavorable outcome was 59% with early surgery and 62% with initial conservative care, a non-significant difference. Results were compatible with a small survival advantage in selected patients, while substantial crossover complicates interpretation.
**Product use:** Preserve the narrow population; never apply it to deep, comatose, cerebellar, or intraventricular hemorrhage.

### D3. Beck et al. — SWITCH trial

**Source:** *Decompressive craniectomy plus best medical treatment versus best medical treatment alone for spontaneous severe deep supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage (SWITCH)* (Lancet, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38761811/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2824%2900702-5)
**Summary:** The trial stopped early after funding ended, randomizing 201 selected patients. At 180 days, mRS 5–6 occurred in 44% with surgery versus 58% with medical treatment (adjusted risk ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.59–1.01; p=0.057), providing weak, inconclusive evidence; severe disability was common in both arms.
**Product use:** Display “possible survival/severe-dependence benefit; uncertainty crosses no effect,” never a definitive benefit claim.

### D4. Cochrane — 2025 surgery for spontaneous supratentorial ICH

**Source:** *Surgery for spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage* (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40673401/)
**Summary:** This review searched through 11 March 2025 and synthesized craniotomy, minimally invasive evacuation, and decompressive craniectomy trials. Effects differ by technique and population; conventional craniotomy does not show a universal functional benefit, while several minimally invasive strategies appear more promising but remain technique- and location-specific.
**Product use:** Use as the umbrella synthesis and prevent cross-technique extrapolation.

### D5. Pradilla et al. — ENRICH

**Source:** *Trial of Early Minimally Invasive Removal of Intracerebral Hemorrhage* (New England Journal of Medicine, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598795/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2308440)
**Summary:** In 300 patients treated within 24 hours, minimally invasive trans-sulcal evacuation improved 180-day utility-weighted mRS overall; the effect was seen in lobar hemorrhage, not the basal-ganglia subgroup. Thirty-day death was 9.3% versus 18.0%, and postoperative rebleeding occurred in 3.3%.
**Product use:** A comparator/evolving option only; do not label it evidence for traditional open craniotomy or every ICH location.

### D6. Hanley et al. — MISTIE III

**Source:** *Efficacy and safety of minimally invasive surgery with thrombolysis in intracerebral haemorrhage evacuation (MISTIE III)* (Lancet, 2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30739747/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2819%2930195-3)
**Summary:** In 506 patients, catheter evacuation plus alteplase did not significantly improve the primary endpoint of mRS 0–3 at one year (45% versus 41%). The procedure met prespecified safety expectations, and exploratory findings linked better clot removal with outcome, but such analyses are not randomized comparisons of achieved evacuation.
**Product use:** Explain that a plausible mechanism and successful clot reduction do not guarantee a proven functional benefit.

### D7. Hanley et al. — CLEAR III

**Source:** *Thrombolytic removal of intraventricular haemorrhage in treatment of severe stroke (CLEAR III)* (Lancet, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28081952/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6108339/)
**Summary:** Among patients with an EVD and obstructing intraventricular blood, alteplase irrigation did not significantly increase mRS 0–3 at 180 days (48% versus 45%). Mortality fell (18% versus 29%), while mRS 5 was more common (17% versus 9%), making survival-versus-disability framing essential.
**Product use:** Never display a mortality reduction without the accompanying functional distribution.

### D8. Kuramatsu et al. — cerebellar ICH IPD observational study

**Source:** *Association of Surgical Hematoma Evacuation vs Conservative Treatment With Functional Outcome in Patients With Cerebellar Intracerebral Hemorrhage* (JAMA, 2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31593272/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2019.13014)
**Summary:** In 578 observational cases, matched analyses found no significant improvement in functional outcome with evacuation overall, but higher survival, particularly for hematomas at least 15 cm³. Exploratory volume analyses suggested possible harm at small volumes; residual confounding and nonrandom treatment remain important.
**Product use:** Supports separate survival and function panels and the guideline's larger-volume rationale, not a stand-alone automated volume rule.

### D9. Sondag et al. — systematic review of cerebellar ICH surgery

**Source:** *Surgical Treatment of Spontaneous Cerebellar Hemorrhage: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis* (2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31848229/)
**Summary:** Forty-one predominantly observational studies (2,062 patients) showed highly heterogeneous outcome estimates and substantial risk of selection bias. The review underscores the absence of randomized evidence despite entrenched emergency practice.
**Product use:** Label cerebellar recommendations as guideline-backed with observational outcome evidence.

### D10. Esquenazi et al. — decompressive craniectomy with or without evacuation

**Source:** *Decompressive hemicraniectomy with or without clot evacuation for large spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral hemorrhages* (Clinical Neurology and Neurosurgery, 2015). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25496934/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clineuro.2014.11.015)
**Summary:** A retrospective cohort of 73 severe ICH patients explored decompression with or without clot evacuation and identified the feasibility of survival. Lack of randomization, small subgroups, and evolving selection practices prevent an efficacy conclusion.
**Product use:** Historical observational support only; SWITCH and current guidelines take precedence.

### D11. 2024 network meta-analysis of surgical strategies

**Source:** *Surgical interventions for spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage: a systematic review and network meta-analysis* (EClinicalMedicine, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39720609/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102999)
**Summary:** Network comparison suggested some minimally invasive approaches may outperform medical management or conventional craniotomy on selected endpoints. Indirect comparisons, variable techniques, timing, location, and trial quality mean rankings should not be treated as a bedside treatment hierarchy.
**Product use:** Never build a league table of operations from network ranks alone.

### D12. 2024 stereotactic aspiration meta-analysis for primary brainstem hemorrhage

**Source:** *Stereotactic aspiration versus conservative treatment for primary brainstem hemorrhage: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (Clinical Neurology and Neurosurgery, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39395282/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clineuro.2024.108583)
**Summary:** Non-randomized studies associated stereotactic aspiration with lower mortality and better function, but treatment selection, regional practice, and study quality create a high risk of bias. Brainstem evacuation remains emerging and is not standard open-surgery guidance.
**Product use:** Keep outside the default pathway and clearly mark investigational/center-specific status.

## E. Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage

### E1. Molyneux et al. — ISAT

**Source:** *International Subarachnoid Aneurysm Trial (ISAT) of neurosurgical clipping versus endovascular coiling* (Lancet, 2002). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12414200/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736%2802%2911314-6)
**Summary:** In 2,143 patients whose ruptured aneurysms were considered suitable for either treatment, death or dependency at one year was 23.7% after coiling versus 30.6% after clipping. Most participants had good clinical grade and small anterior-circulation aneurysms, so the result does not cover every anatomy or patients needing open hematoma evacuation.
**Product use:** State “when both are suitable in patients like those studied,” not “coiling is always safer.”

### E2. Molyneux et al. — ISAT follow-up

**Source:** *International Subarachnoid Aneurysm Trial: long-term follow-up* (Lancet, 2005). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16139655/)
**Summary:** Follow-up confirmed an early functional advantage to coiling in the trial population while documenting rebleeding and retreatment considerations. Treatment durability and long-term surveillance remain part of an honest comparison.
**Product use:** Include “future imaging/possible retreatment” rather than ending the story at hospital discharge.

### E3. Spetzler et al. — BRAT 10-year results

**Source:** *The Barrow Ruptured Aneurysm Trial: 10-year results and analysis* (Journal of Neurosurgery, 2019). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30849758/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3171/2018.8.JNS181846)
**Summary:** At ten years, clinical outcomes no longer significantly differed between assigned strategies, while clipping had much higher complete obliteration and far lower retreatment. Large crossover—especially from coiling assignment to clipping—and single-center expertise complicate intention-to-treat interpretation.
**Product use:** Explain the tradeoff between early invasiveness, anatomical suitability, durable occlusion, and retreatment.

### E4. Cochrane — coiling versus clipping

**Source:** *Endovascular coiling versus neurosurgical clipping for people with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage* (Cochrane review, 2018). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30110521/)
**Summary:** Randomized evidence supports coiling for lower death/dependency in aneurysms suitable for both treatments, but applicability is constrained by aneurysm and patient selection. Evidence about durability, posterior circulation, poor-grade patients, and modern devices is less certain.
**Product use:** Show an eligibility gate before presenting comparative numbers.

### E5. 2022 clipping-versus-coiling meta-analysis

**Source:** *Clipping versus coiling for ruptured intracranial aneurysms: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (Neurosurgical Review, 2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34870768/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8976818/)
**Summary:** Pooled studies found tradeoffs: clipping was associated with more complete occlusion and less rebleeding/retreatment, while coiling was associated with fewer poor outcomes or some complications in selected populations. Mixing randomized and observational evidence, eras, aneurysm sites, and technologies limits any single summary effect.
**Product use:** Build a tradeoff view, not a winner display.

### E6. Alotaibi et al. — decompressive craniectomy after poor-grade aSAH

**Source:** *Decompressive craniectomy for patients with poor-grade aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (Journal of Neurosurgery, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28059660/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3171/2016.9.JNS161383)
**Summary:** Fifteen observational studies (407 patients) reported poor functional outcome in roughly 61% and death in roughly 28% after decompression. Without reliable non-surgical controls, these figures describe selected survivors and cannot prove benefit or predict a new patient.
**Product use:** Rescue-decompression outcomes may appear only with a high-bias warning.

### E7. Darkwah Oppong et al. — primary decompression in aSAH

**Source:** *Primary decompressive craniectomy after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage—single-center study and systematic review* (Neurosurgical Review, 2021). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32920754/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8338868/)
**Summary:** A retrospective cohort and literature synthesis found no clear favorable-outcome improvement from primary decompression after adjustment, though selected patients recovered. Confounding by initial severity and hematoma burden remains profound.
**Product use:** Describe primary decompression as selected rescue practice, not routine adjunct to clipping.

## F. Cerebral venous thrombosis

### F1. Ferro et al. — decompressive surgery registry

**Source:** *Decompressive surgery in cerebrovenous thrombosis: a multicenter registry and a systematic review of individual patient data* (Stroke, 2011). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21799156/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.111.615393)
**Summary:** Among 69 severe cases compiled from a registry and literature, 56.5% achieved mRS 0–2 and 15.9% died. Publication, selection, and survivor bias are substantial, but the series established that impending herniation from CVT is not uniformly futile.
**Product use:** Say meaningful recovery is possible; do not quote 56.5% as the patient's chance.

### F2. Zuurbier et al. — prospective case series

**Source:** *Decompressive hemicraniectomy in severe cerebral venous thrombosis: a prospective case series* (Journal of Neurology, 2012). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22119770/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3366184/)
**Summary:** Eight of ten consecutively enrolled patients survived and six had good outcomes, supporting emergency decompression despite severe signs. The series is too small and uncontrolled to establish effect size or selection criteria.
**Product use:** Use as proof of possible recovery after an alarming early presentation, with explicit uncertainty.

### F3. Aaron et al. — single-center 44-patient series

**Source:** *Decompressive craniectomy in cerebral venous thrombosis: a single-centre experience* (Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery & Psychiatry, 2013). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23591554/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2012-303356)
**Summary:** In 44 surgically treated patients, mortality was about 20%; most survivors achieved mRS 0–2 at follow-up. Referral patterns, local selection, and no medical-control group limit generalization.
**Product use:** Demonstrate long recovery potential, not individualized probabilities.

### F4. Aaron et al. — DECOMPRESS2 observational study

**Source:** *Outcomes of Decompressive Surgery for Patients With Severe Cerebral Venous Thrombosis: DECOMPRESS2 Observational Study* (Stroke, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38572636/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.123.045051)
**Summary:** In this prospective international cohort of 118 operated patients, 33.9% had died and 35.6% were independent at 12 months. Among survivors, 78.9% of patients and 87.1% of caregivers judged surgery positively. The study improves contemporary description but remains nonrandomized and its survivor-only opinion results are vulnerable to selection, adaptation, and nonresponse bias.
**Product use:** Prefer its patient/caregiver-centered outcomes over older case reports, while retaining the observational label.

### F5. Coutinho et al. — DECOMPRESS2 quality of life

**Source:** *Long-term quality of life after decompressive surgery for severe cerebral venous thrombosis* (Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases, 2026). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41500360/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jstrokecerebrovasdis.2026.108549)
**Summary:** Among 112 participants, median EQ-5D index was 0.70 and median self-rated health 70/100; 16% reported full health and 9% a negative quality-of-life value. Group averages hide wide individual variation and survivor/nonresponse effects.
**Product use:** Quality of life is multidimensional and cannot be inferred from mRS alone.

## G. Ruptured brain AVM

### G1. Beecher et al. — delayed treatment and rebleeding

**Source:** *Delayed treatment of ruptured brain arteriovenous malformations: is it ok to wait?* (World Neurosurgery, 2020). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31678312/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2019.10.148)
**Summary:** A retrospective series examined rehemorrhage during planned delay before definitive AVM treatment. Delay may permit physiological stabilization and operative planning in selected patients, but associated aneurysms and other high-risk features can change the balance; selection prevents causal timing claims.
**Product use:** Show “treat pressure now” and “cure the AVM now or later” as separate, anatomy-dependent decisions.

### G2. 2021 low-grade AVM microsurgery systematic review

**Source:** *Microsurgery for low-grade brain arteriovenous malformations: a systematic review* (2021). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34562139/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00234-021-02714-x)
**Summary:** Surgical series report high obliteration rates for selected low-grade AVMs, but most evidence is retrospective, from expert centers, and mixes ruptured and unruptured cases. Low grade does not mean low-risk emergency surgery for every patient.
**Product use:** Never infer acute suitability from grade alone.

### G3. 2022 preoperative embolization systematic review

**Source:** *Preoperative embolisation of brain arteriovenous malformations: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35260972/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9160113/)
**Summary:** Evidence did not establish that routine embolization before microsurgery improves outcomes, and embolization itself introduces neurological and hemorrhagic risks. Techniques and selection vary markedly across centers.
**Product use:** Do not animate embolization as a universally required first step.

### G4. 2024 early-versus-delayed ruptured-AVM surgery cohort

**Source:** *Early versus delayed microsurgical resection of ruptured brain arteriovenous malformations* (World Neurosurgery, 2024). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39168243/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wneu.2024.08.073)
**Summary:** Retrospective timing comparisons reflect strong selection by hemorrhage severity, AVM complexity, emergency mass effect, and clinical stability. They may help generate center protocols but cannot establish a universal timing rule.
**Product use:** Force clinician selection of a patient-specific rationale; do not auto-recommend “early” or “delayed.”

## H. Craniectomy complications, cranioplasty, and recovery

### H1. Kurland et al. — decompressive craniectomy complications review

**Source:** *Complications Associated with Decompressive Craniectomy: A Systematic Review* (Neurocritical Care, 2015). [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4704457/)
**Summary:** Complications cluster over time: early expansion/new bleeding and extracranial herniation; later cerebrospinal-fluid disturbances, infection, seizures, and syndrome of the trephined; and cranioplasty-related complications. Rates vary by indication, technique, surveillance, and definitions.
**Product use:** Organize risks by phase and avoid one pooled “surgical risk” number.

### H2. Ashayeri et al. — syndrome of the trephined systematic review

**Source:** *Syndrome of the Trephined: A Systematic Review* (Neurosurgery, 2016). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27489166/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1227/NEU.0000000000001366)
**Summary:** Neurological, cognitive, headache, fatigue, or postural symptoms can appear or worsen in association with a large skull defect and may improve after cranioplasty. The literature is mainly case reports/series with inconsistent definitions.
**Product use:** Explain why later reconstruction may have functional as well as protective/cosmetic goals, without promising recovery.

### H3. Piedra et al. — early versus late cranioplasty after stroke

**Source:** *Timing of cranioplasty after decompressive craniectomy for ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke* (Journal of Neurosurgery, 2013). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23140156/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3171/2012.10.JNS121037)
**Summary:** In this retrospective cohort, overall complication rates did not significantly differ between early and later cranioplasty, while shunt dependence predicted complications. Selection and a modest sample prevent a definitive timing rule.
**Product use:** Describe timing as dependent on swelling, infection, wound condition, hydrocephalus, material, and local practice.

### H4. Goedemans et al. — stroke cranioplasty cohort

**Source:** *Complications in cranioplasty after decompressive craniectomy for stroke* (Journal of Neurology, 2020). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31953606/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7184041/)
**Summary:** In 145 patients, cranioplasty complications occurred in about 23%, with risk varying by factors such as hydrocephalus and timing/context. Retrospective definitions and center practice limit transportability.
**Product use:** Use the categories to prepare families, not the aggregate rate as individualized risk.

### H5. 2025 cranioplasty timing systematic review

**Source:** *The timing of cranioplasty and postoperative complications: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (EClinicalMedicine, 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41181848/)
**Summary:** Across 65 heterogeneous studies, timing associations differed by complication and patient context; non-random allocation and inconsistent “early” definitions remain major limitations.
**Product use:** Do not display a universal optimal week for reconstruction.

### H6. 2021 cranioplasty material meta-analysis

**Source:** *Complications following cranioplasty and relationship to implant material: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (2021). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34100535/)
**Summary:** Revision, infection, exposure, and resorption risks differ across studies and materials, but comparisons are confounded by indication, defect size, storage practice, and follow-up.
**Product use:** Material choice requires surgeon/institution input; a generic “own bone is safer” or “synthetic is safer” claim is not supported.

## I. Communication, consent, and family decision support

### I1. Holloway et al. — prognostication and shared decision-making in neurocritical care

**Source:** *Prognostication and shared decision making in neurocritical care* (BMJ, 2022). [Full text](https://www.bmj.com/content/377/bmj-2021-060154) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2021-060154)
**Summary:** The review integrates calibrated prognosis, uncertainty, time-limited trials, surrogate decision-making, disability bias, and goals-of-care communication after severe brain injury. It emphasizes that clinicians should make a values-informed recommendation rather than transfer an unstructured burden to families.
**Product use:** Core conceptual basis for the conversation workflow and best/most likely/worst-case display.

### I2. Goss and Creutzfeldt — communicating with families after severe acute brain injury

**Source:** *Communicating with Families of Patients with Severe Acute Brain Injury* (Neurocritical Care, 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40214887/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12028-025-02252-x)
**Summary:** The review addresses early uncertainty, surrogate distress, values elicitation, recommendation-making, repeated meetings, and communication frameworks tailored to brain injury. Communication is a longitudinal process, not one consent encounter.
**Product use:** Require a follow-up checkpoint and a persistent record of questions/values rather than a one-time “completed” state.

### I3. Kiker et al. — qualitative meta-synthesis of family communication in severe acute brain injury

**Source:** *Family Experiences of Communication in Severe Acute Brain Injury: A Qualitative Meta-Synthesis* (Neurocritical Care, 2025). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39192102/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11950011/)
**Summary:** Eighteen qualitative studies identify recurring family needs: understandable and consistent information, emotional recognition, honesty without abandonment, participation, access to clinicians, and help tolerating uncertainty. Experiences differ by culture, relationship, and clinical trajectory.
**Product use:** Design for repeated orientation, team consistency, empathy prompts, and question capture—not information density alone.

### I4. Muehlschlegel et al. — shared decision-making bundle

**Source:** *A Shared Decision-Making Intervention for Goals-of-Care Decisions in the NeuroICU* (Neurology: Clinical Practice, 2023). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36865641/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9973293/)
**Summary:** A structured bundle using premeeting huddles, a trained social worker, family meetings, and documentation increased documentation of communication processes from 27% to 83% in a before-after implementation study. It did not prove better patient outcomes and required human facilitation.
**Product use:** Implement checklist and documentation support around—not instead of—the family meeting.

### I5. Muehlschlegel et al. — decision-aid pilot for severe acute brain injury

**Source:** *A Decision Aid Intervention for Family Surrogates of Patients with Severe Acute Brain Injury* (Neurology, 2022). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35853748/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9576301/)
**Summary:** A paper-based decision aid was feasible and acceptable in pilot testing and supported structured discussion of prognosis and goals. A pilot cannot establish clinical effectiveness, and a generalized SABI aid is not equivalent to procedure-specific informed consent.
**Product use:** Evidence that structured aids can be usable; require comparative testing before claiming benefit from immersive delivery.

### I6. Weber et al. — family dissatisfaction after severe brain injury

**Source:** *Predictors of Family Dissatisfaction with Support During Neurocritical Care Shared Decision-Making* (Neurocritical Care, 2021). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33821402/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8021441/)
**Summary:** Family dissatisfaction was associated with aspects of communication exposure, including having few formal family meetings. Observational associations do not define a magic meeting count, but reinforce continuity and access.
**Product use:** Prompt a next meeting, responsible clinician, and contact route before ending a session.

### I7. Quinn et al. — surrogate preference for numeric prognostic information

**Source:** *Surrogate Decision Makers' Expectations of and Perspectives on Prognostic Information after Severe Traumatic Brain Injury* (Neurocritical Care, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28685395/) · [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5693603/)
**Summary:** Most surrogates wanted numeric prognostic information, but also needed explanation, honesty, and context. The study concerns traumatic—not stroke—brain injury, so the communication preference is transferable only cautiously.
**Product use:** Offer an optional clinician-selected numeric layer using matched evidence; never force numbers or fabricate precision.

### I8. Zahuranec et al. — surrogate perspectives after ICH

**Source:** *Surrogate decision makers' perspectives on family members' prognosis after intracerebral hemorrhage* (2018). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29608394/)
**Summary:** Surrogates combine clinician information with faith, knowledge of the patient, visible signs, hope, and prior experiences when interpreting prognosis. Simply presenting more data will not resolve value conflict or uncertainty.
**Product use:** Invite the family to explain what they are hearing, hoping, fearing, and using to make sense of the situation.

### I9. Becker et al. — withdrawal of support and self-fulfilling prophecy

**Source:** *Withdrawal of support in intracerebral hemorrhage may lead to self-fulfilling prophecies* (Neurology, 2001). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11274312/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.56.6.766)
**Summary:** Early care limitations were strongly associated with death and could distort outcome models, illustrating how predicted poor outcome may help cause the observed outcome. The older observational study cannot specify when limitation is appropriate.
**Product use:** Display an early-prognosis uncertainty warning and prevent severity scores from producing treatment-limit recommendations.

### I10. Weimer et al. — treatment limitation and outcome modeling after ICH

**Source:** *Assessment of Withdrawal of Care Bias in the Prediction of Intracerebral Hemorrhage Mortality* (Critical Care Medicine, 2016). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26807687/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000001570)
**Summary:** This work further analyzes how withdrawal practices and prognosis interact, showing that adjustment is difficult and simplistic interpretations are unsafe. Not all poor outcomes are caused by care limits, and appropriate values-based limits remain part of good care.
**Product use:** Use nuanced language: avoid premature certainty without implying that comfort-focused decisions are wrong.

### I11. Taylor et al. — Best Case/Worst Case communication tool

**Source:** *A Framework to Improve Surgeon Communication in High-Stakes Surgical Decisions: Best Case/Worst Case* (JAMA Surgery, 2017). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28146230/)
**Summary:** A proof-of-concept intervention trained surgeons to describe best and worst outcomes, identify the most likely trajectory, and make a recommendation on a visual aid; observed shared-decision communication scores improved. The work involved high-risk acute surgical decisions in older adults, not validation for emergency stroke consent.
**Product use:** Adapt the branching-story structure with stroke-specific evidence and clinical review; do not copy it as a validated stroke script.

### I12. Lautrette et al. — VALUE-based ICU family conference intervention

**Source:** *A Communication Strategy and Brochure for Relatives of Patients Dying in the ICU* (New England Journal of Medicine, 2007). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17267907/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa063446)
**Summary:** A structured family conference and bereavement brochure reduced family anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic-stress symptoms after ICU deaths. The mnemonic emphasizes valuing statements, acknowledging emotion, listening, understanding the patient as a person, and eliciting questions; the trial context was end-of-life ICU care, not surgical consent.
**Product use:** Add empathy/listening prompts without implying psychological benefit from the app alone.

### I13. Baile et al. — SPIKES framework

**Source:** *SPIKES—A Six-Step Protocol for Delivering Bad News* (The Oncologist, 2000). [Full text](https://academic.oup.com/oncolo/article/5/4/302/6386019) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1634/theoncologist.5-4-302)
**Summary:** SPIKES structures setting, perception, invitation, knowledge, empathy, and strategy/summary. It was developed in oncology and is a communication scaffold rather than outcome-proven emergency-neurosurgical consent.
**Product use:** Borrow warning-shot, information-chunking, and emotion-response behaviors; use the stroke workflow in this library for the actual sequence.

### I14. AHRQ — teach-back

**Source:** Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, *Tool: Teach-Back*. [Official resource](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/teachback.html)
**Summary:** Teach-back asks the recipient to explain information in their own words so the clinician can identify what they need to explain more clearly. It is a test of the explanation, not of the family's intelligence, and should not sound like an exam.
**Product use:** End every module with a clinician-delivered teach-back prompt and a repair loop.

### I15. AHRQ — Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit

**Source:** Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, *Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit, 3rd edition*. [Official resource](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/index.html)
**Summary:** The toolkit assumes any person may have difficulty understanding health information, especially under stress, and recommends plain language, limited key points, teach-back, usable written material, and supportive systems.
**Product use:** Default to low cognitive load; never make a family request a “simplified mode.”

### I16. GMC — seven principles of decision making and consent

**Source:** General Medical Council, *The seven principles of decision making and consent*. [Official guidance](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/decision-making-and-consent/the-seven-principles-of-decision-making-and-consent)
**Summary:** Consent is a meaningful dialogue about benefits, harms, alternatives, and what matters to the patient, not a signature. Adults are presumed to have capacity; clinicians must support involvement, respect refusal, and tailor material information.
**Product use:** The application may support dialogue but cannot capture a tap and label it informed consent.

### I17. GMC — emergency circumstances affecting decisions

**Source:** General Medical Council, *Circumstances that affect the decision-making process*. [Official guidance](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/decision-making-and-consent/circumstances-that-affect-the-decision-making-process)
**Summary:** In an emergency, a conscious patient is still presumed to have capacity and should be involved. If a patient lacks capacity and wishes cannot be determined, immediately necessary treatment may proceed under applicable law and professional duties; family roles differ by legal jurisdiction and appointment status.
**Product use:** Require jurisdiction-specific configuration; never state that “the closest relative gives consent” as a universal rule.

### I18. American College of Surgeons — informed consent

**Source:** American College of Surgeons, *Informed Consent*. [Patient resource](https://www.facs.org/for-patients/preparing-for-surgery/informed-consent/) · [ACS principles](https://www.facs.org/about-acs/statements/statements-on-principles/)
**Summary:** The operating surgeon is responsible for a comprehensible discussion of diagnosis, nature/purpose of the operation, risks, benefits, alternatives, likely consequences, and questions. Delegation and emergency exceptions are constrained by professional and local rules.
**Product use:** Preserve surgeon ownership and document that the visualization is an adjunct.

### I19. Joint Commission — informed consent and health literacy

**Source:** The Joint Commission, *Quick Safety 21: Informed consent—more than getting a signature* (updated 2022). [PDF](https://www.jointcommission.org/-/media/tjc/newsletters/quick-safety-21-update-4-4-22.pdf)
**Summary:** Consent failures commonly arise from complex language, inadequate interpretation, power imbalance, and failure to verify understanding. The resource recommends plain language, qualified interpreters, and teach-back.
**Product use:** Include an interpreter check and understanding repair loop before any documentation state.

### I20. HHS Office of Minority Health — National CLAS Standards

**Source:** US Department of Health and Human Services, *National Culturally and Linguistically Appropriate Services Standards*. [Official standards](https://thinkculturalhealth.hhs.gov/clas/standards)
**Summary:** Organizations should provide timely, competent language assistance at no cost, notify people of its availability, and avoid untrained individuals or minors as interpreters. Communication should be respectful of language, health literacy, culture, and disability.
**Product use:** Interpreter mode must support—not replace—a qualified human interpreter and must never rely on machine translation for consent-critical meaning without validation.

## Reading the catalogue responsibly

- The newest source is not automatically the strongest; a current guideline may appropriately rely on an older pivotal trial.
- A statistically non-significant trial does not prove treatments are equivalent, and an observational association does not prove benefit.
- A mortality benefit can coexist with more survivors in states of severe dependence.
- A group mRS outcome cannot represent language, cognition, identity, caregiver burden, quality of life, or the patient's own view of an acceptable life.
- Source summaries should be reviewed whenever an interface phrase, number, model state, or animation is derived from them.
