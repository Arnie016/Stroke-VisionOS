## V3 interaction grammar

1. **Summon:** the person explicitly opens any of the three lenses. Nothing is
   permanently forced into view.
2. **Inspect:** gaze/pinch focuses one open lens and brings it to a comfortable
   central inspection locus.
3. **Enter:** the selected vessel world expands around the stationary person;
   the registered overview recedes but remains a faint reference.
4. **Return:** one control restores the atlas overview without closing saved lenses.
5. **Dismiss:** each lens can close independently; one command closes all.

## Information hierarchy

- One title at the top names the current spatial relationship.
- One explanatory paragraph describes what is visible and labels its limits.
- One “Did you know?” fact adds bounded context.
- One local Save action bookmarks the exact station or portal lesson. No
  account or patient data is stored.
- Navigation and portal agency remain in a lower hand-adjacent control surface.

## Judge-facing exhibit route

The default three-minute route is a separate presentation state, not a removal
of the seven-station atlas:

0. **Threshold:** four calm, user-paced readings establish anatomy, the
   blockage-to-territory problem, and the invitation. A distant brain enlarges
   until the cortical shell becomes the environment. The person may go back,
   skip, or use Reduce Motion; no camera ride is used.
1. **Route:** living flow and a portal-projected causal trail make the Circle
   to example right-M1 teaching relationship discoverable.
2. **Blockage:** flow holds, the named obstruction becomes the visual focus,
   and the same Circle portal moves to the inspection locus.
3. **Consequence:** the Circle region enlarges and an illustrative downstream
   facet field reveals that the event continues beyond the clot.

Exactly one portal is used in this route. The person chooses the journey before
it appears, can move backward, restart, reveal or hide the bounded fact, save a
beat, or exit. The open atlas retains independent control of all three portals.

This adapts the museum principle of choosing an entry and learning through a
responsive environment. It does not copy Mirror Mirror artwork, branding,
rooms, audio, text, or interaction assets.

The four entry readings use only floating type and compact controls. Their
purpose is not to deliver a lecture. They make the spatial transition legible:
first the brain is an object ahead, then it becomes the place from which the
vascular lesson is understood.

## Region portal reel

The broader atlas is presented as a lower episode rail rather than six portals
floating around the wearer. Three destinations are visible at a time, the rail
scrolls for more, and one transfer is active at a time. Lumen, Circle, Exchange,
Ventricles, Cerebellum, and Deep Brain are backed by named bundled USDZ content.
The cortical vault remains as environmental context during transfer.

## Art-science direction

The double-sided cortical vault is the brain-scale room; no second brain model
floats in front of the wearer. The surrounding identity echo field uses
original, low-contrast facets as a metaphor for memory, self, and changing
perspective. It does not encode consciousness level, diagnosis, neural firing,
emotion, identity, or mental state.

## Medical content boundary

- The Circle lens shows a generic anastomotic connection between anterior and
  posterior cerebral circulation; anatomy varies between people.
- The Exchange lens shows oxygen moving from capillary blood toward tissue;
  red cells remain intravascular.
- The lesson is not patient-specific, not CFD, not outcome prediction, and not
  a treatment or diagnostic tool.
